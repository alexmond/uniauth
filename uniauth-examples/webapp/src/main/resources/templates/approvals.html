Who was refused

A denied principal gets a plain 403 rather than the waiting page, and never turns up in the queue again — so without this table a refusal was final by accident. Reconsidering is forgetting them: the next request they make puts them back at the top of the page.

Approval is keyed on the identifier in grey — a subject claim, for an OIDC login — because that is stable and never reassigned, while an address can change hands. The name and address above it are what the provider said at first sighting, kept so the decision is about a person rather than a number. An address marked unverified is one the provider will not stand behind.

This queue lives in memory, so it empties on restart — and every approval goes with it. A real deployment supplies its own ApprovalStore.
