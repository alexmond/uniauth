One chain,
two kinds of door.

The obvious way to support four mechanisms is four filter chains. It does not work: Spring Security matches chains in order and stops at the first one, so three of them would never run. UniAuth builds a single chain and installs form login, OAuth2 login and SAML login onto it together.

Form-based: internal and LDAP

These two share one username and password form. They are told apart after the fact, by the AuthenticationProvider chain: each contributes a provider, and Spring Security tries them in order until one accepts the credentials. That is what lets a deployment keep a couple of local break-glass accounts next to a corporate directory.

Redirect-based: OAuth2 and SAML

These keep their own entry points — /oauth2/authorization/{id} and /saml2/authenticate/{id} — so each registration is just a link. The chooser reads them from the repositories Spring Boot already built from spring.security.oauth2.client.* and spring.security.saml2.relyingparty.*; UniAuth does not re-declare that configuration.

What this sample runs

The internal store and an in-process LDAP directory, both enabled out of the box so sign-in works with no external services. OAuth2 and SAML need a real identity provider — application.yaml carries commented blocks showing where their configuration goes.
